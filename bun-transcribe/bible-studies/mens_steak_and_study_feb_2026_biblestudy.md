# Awakening to Godly Strength: From Eden's Failure to the Cross's Victory

## The Anchor Text
Have your Bible open to **Genesis chapters 1-3**, **Romans 5:19**, **Ephesians 5:25**, and **1 Corinthians 16:13**.

## Contextual Investigation
1. Read Genesis 1:26-27 alongside Genesis 3. What was God's original design for humanity's roles and relationship before sin entered? How does the setting of the Garden of Eden make the events that follow stand out?
2. Consider the church in Corinth when Paul wrote 1 Corinthians. What kinds of struggles (like division, immorality, or weakness) might make his command in 1 Corinthians 16:13 urgently needed?
3. Look at Romans 5 in context. Who is called the "first Adam," and how does Jesus as the "second Adam" enter the picture amid humanity's brokenness?

## Discovery Questions (Observation & Interpretation)
### Observing the Problem in the Fall (Genesis 3)
1. In Genesis 3:6, what exactly happens involving the woman, the fruit, and the man who is "with her"? What does his response (or lack of it) reveal?
2. Scan Genesis 3:12 and 3:16-19. What patterns of blame, conflict, and distorted relationships emerge right after the disobedience? How do these twist God's created order from Genesis 1:26-27?

### Observing the Person Who Restores (Romans 5:19; Ephesians 5:25)
3. Compare Adam's actions in Genesis 3 with what Romans 5:19 says about "the one man Jesus Christ." What specific obedience does Jesus model that reverses the failure?
4. In Ephesians 5:25, what bold command is given to husbands about their role toward their wives? How does this picture reflect Christ's own steps forward?

### Observing the Practice of True Strength (1 Corinthians 16:13)
5. Break down each command in 1 Corinthians 16:13—"Be watchful... stand firm... act like men... be strong." What threats, anchors, actions, and sources of power does the text point to?
6. Across these verses, what repeated themes of initiative, endurance, and sacrifice show up? How do they connect back to the failures in Genesis 3 and Christ's victory?

## Deep Reflection (Heart-Work)
Take time in silence to journal or pray through these:
1. Where in your own life—family, work, faith—do you notice echoes of Adam's silence or withdrawal? Why do you think that's showing up there?
2. When have you shifted blame or avoided hard leadership, like in the Garden? What fears or comforts keep you from stepping forward like Christ?
3. What "dragons" (habits of passivity, avoidance, or compromise) are you tolerating right now? Why haven't you confronted them yet?
4. If 1 Corinthians 16:13 defined your daily strength, what would change first in how you lead and love others?

## The Discovery Challenge (Application)
This week, identify one specific area of passivity or avoidance in your life (like a conversation, habit, or leadership step). Emulate Christ's obedience by initiating sacrificial action there—pray through it daily, then obey without delay, and share the outcome with an accountable brother.
